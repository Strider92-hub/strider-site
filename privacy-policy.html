Strider · Last updated 13 October 2026

Strider is a scheduling application for individual content creators. It publishes a creator's own videos to the creator's own social media accounts at times they choose. This policy explains exactly what data Strider accesses, how it is used, who it is shared with, how it is protected, and how long it is kept.

The short version. Strider keeps what it needs to run your account and publish for you: your email address, the access tokens for the accounts you connect, the videos and pictures you upload (in your library until you delete them), your captions and schedule, and a record of what was published. It does not read your messages, your followers or your private data. The only figures it reads are the view, like, comment and repost counts of posts Strider published for you on YouTube and X (and, once you allow it, public TikTok videos and your TikTok follower count), to show you how they are doing. It publishes only to the accounts you connect, and only what you schedule. Disconnecting an account deletes its access straight away; closing your Strider account deletes everything Strider holds about you.
Strider uses YouTube API Services. Publishing to YouTube is done through YouTube's own official interfaces. Google's handling of your data is covered by the Google Privacy Policy, and the YouTube Terms of Service apply to what you publish there.

You can remove Strider's access to your Google account at any time — whether or not you keep using Strider — at the Google security settings page.

1. What data Strider accesses

From the social media platforms

When you connect an account, Strider requests the narrowest permissions that allow it to publish. It requests no permission it does not use.

PlatformWhat Strider accessesWhy
YouTube Permission to upload video to your channel. Read-only access to your channel's name and ID, and to the view, like and comment counts of videos Strider published for you. Uploading is the core function. The channel name and ID are read immediately before every upload and compared with the channel you set up, so a video cannot be published to the wrong channel. The counts are shown only to you, on your Post performance page.

If you allow it on TikTok's own permission screen: the view, like, comment and share counts of your public videos, and your follower count. Publishing is the core function. Instagram Permission to publish to your Instagram Business or Creator account, its username and account ID, and the linked Facebook Page. Instagram publishing requires the linked Page. The username and ID confirm the destination before each post.
Facebook Permission to publish to Pages you manage, and the list and names of those Pages. The Page list is shown so you can choose which Page to publish to.
X Permission to post, your account username, and the view, like, reply and repost counts of posts Strider published for you. Posting is the core function. The username confirms the destination. The counts are shown only to you, on your Post performance page.

Personal profiles are never connected. Strider can only connect Facebook Pages you manage and Instagram Business or Creator accounts, because those are the only kinds Meta allows apps to publish to. It never connects to, reads or posts to a personal Facebook profile or a personal Instagram account.

Strider does not access: your direct messages, your followers or who you follow, your audience data or any analytics beyond the counts described above, other people's posts, your email, your contacts, your location, or anything belonging to any account you have not connected.

From you

  • Your email address and, if you give it, your name
  • Your password, stored only as a scrambled hash that cannot be turned back into the password (not stored at all if you only sign in with Google or Facebook)
  • The videos and pictures you upload, kept in your library until you delete them or close your account
  • Your captions, titles, text posts, links, settings for each post and the times you choose, and the plans you add to your calendar
  • A record of what was published and when, so the app can show you your history and avoid publishing the same thing twice
  • A short identifier of each video's contents, so the same clip is not accidentally published twice to the same account. It is derived from the file and cannot be turned back into it
  • Your plan and billing record from Paddle: your Paddle customer and subscription references, the plan, its status and dates, and confirmation that a payment went through or was refunded. Strider never sees or stores your card details
  • Your X balance: top-ups and what each X post cost
  • If you take part: what you post in the Community (with the display name you choose), your votes and any reports you make, and, for ambassadors, referral and promotional-post records
  • Messages Strider sends to your in-app inbox, and whether you have read them

What the Strider team can see

Strider is run by one person. To run the service and help you, the owner's dashboard shows each member's email address and name, plan, storage used, number of posts, which platforms are connected (with public account names where available), Community activity and ambassador status. It never shows access tokens, passwords, or the content of your uploads. Changes the owner makes to an account (for example adding posts to your allowance or changing your plan) are logged with who made them and when.

If you sign in with Google

You can create your Strider account or sign in with your Google account instead of a password. Google then tells Strider your name, your email address, whether Google has confirmed that address, and an identifier for your Google account. Strider uses these only to create your account and sign you in. It never sees your Google password and does not access anything else in your Google account. This sign-in is separate from connecting YouTube, which is described above. Deleting your Strider account deletes this link too.

If you sign in with Facebook

You can also create your Strider account or sign in with your Facebook account. Facebook then tells Strider your name, your email address (if you have one on Facebook) and an identifier for your Facebook account. Strider uses these only to create your account and sign you in. Before an account is made, Strider sends a code to the email address you choose, to confirm it is yours. Strider never sees your Facebook password, does not access anything else in your Facebook account, and never posts to Facebook because you signed in this way. This sign-in is separate from connecting a Facebook Page or Instagram account to post to, which is described above. Deleting your Strider account deletes this link too.

If you turn on two-step sign-in

Two-step sign-in is optional. If you turn it on in your account settings, Strider stores a secret key, encrypted, that it uses to check the six-digit codes your authenticator app shows, together with your backup codes in a scrambled form that cannot be turned back into the codes. Strider also keeps a count of recent wrong codes, to stop anyone guessing. Your authenticator app works the codes out on your own phone; nothing is sent to it by Strider or anyone else. Turning two-step sign-in off, or deleting your account, deletes all of this.

Cookies

Strider sets one cookie, and only after you sign in.

NameWhat it is forHow long it lasts
strider_session Keeps you signed in, so you are not asked for your password on every page. It holds a random identifier and nothing else — no name, no email address, and nothing about you. 30 days, or until you sign out

It is strictly necessary: without it, signing in would not work at all. That is why there is no cookie banner — there is nothing to opt out of that would leave a working product, and Strider sets no analytics, advertising or tracking cookies of any kind.

The cookie is marked HttpOnly, so scripts on the page cannot read it, and SameSite=Lax, so another website cannot use it to act as you. When Strider is reached over https it is also marked Secure, so it is never sent unencrypted.

Signing out deletes it. Clearing your browser's cookies for this site does the same thing, and signs you out.

2. How that data is used

Only to do the thing you asked for, at the time you asked for it:

  • Publishing. Your video and caption are sent to the platform you chose, at the time you chose.
  • Confirming the destination. Before every post, Strider checks the connected account matches the one you set up. If it does not, the post is refused and you are told.
  • Telling you what happened. Successes and failures are recorded so you can see them, and so a failed post can be retried.
  • Showing you how your posts are doing. For posts Strider published to YouTube and X, it reads their view, like, comment and repost counts and shows them to you. For public TikTok videos, if you allow it, it reads their view, like, comment and share counts and your follower count, in the same way. They are never shown to anyone else, never combined with other people's figures, and never used for anything else.
  • Support. If you send a diagnostic report, it is used to investigate the problem you reported and nothing else.

Your data is not used for advertising, for profiling, for building any audience model, for resale, or for training artificial intelligence or machine learning models. Your videos are not viewed by us except where you specifically ask for help with one.

Also used for

  • Your plan and payments. Paddle's confirmations are used to switch your plan on or off and to work out your allowance.
  • Email about your account. Sign-in and confirmation codes, security notices (for example when two-step sign-in is turned on or off), welcome emails and notices about your plan. Strider does not send marketing email.
  • Keeping Strider safe and working. Limiting repeated wrong passwords and codes, preventing abuse of the platforms, keeping backups, and the server records described in section 4.
  • The Community. What you post there is shown to other Strider members, with your display name. Reports are reviewed by the owner.

3. Who it is shared with

Your data is not sold, rented or shared with anyone for their own purposes. It is not shared with advertisers, data brokers, analytics companies, or AI services.

It is disclosed only in these circumstances:

  • To the platform you chose. Publishing to TikTok means sending your video to TikTok. That is the service working as intended.
  • To the suppliers who run our systems, strictly to provide the service. They act on our instructions and cannot use your data for anything else:
    • Mythic Beasts — the server Strider runs on, in London
    • Namecheap (Private Email) — sends Strider's emails and holds the hello@getstrider.co.uk mailbox
    • GitHub (GitHub Pages) — hosts this public website, so it sees the usual details any website host sees when you visit
  • To Paddle, who sell Strider's paid plans as the seller of record. Paddle collects your payment details directly and handles them under its own privacy policy; Strider receives only the billing record described in section 1.
  • To Google or Meta, only if you choose to sign in with them (see section 1). They already know you are using their sign-in.
  • Where the law requires it.

4. How it is protected

Access tokens

The tokens that allow Strider to publish on your behalf are stored encrypted, are never written to logs, and are never shown on any screen. They are transmitted only to the platform that issued them, over an encrypted connection.

Data in transit

All connections use HTTPS. Videos are uploaded directly to the platforms using their own published upload interfaces.

Your uploads

The videos and pictures you upload stay in your library, so you can post them again, until you delete them or close your account. Files Strider makes only to publish something (for example a converted picture, or a temporary link a platform fetches a picture from) are deleted as soon as that post is finished. Strider is not a backup service: keep your own copies.

Passwords and two-step sign-in

Passwords are stored only as a salted hash. Two-step sign-in secrets are encrypted, and backup codes are stored only in a form that cannot be turned back into the codes.

Backups

The server saves a copy of Strider's database every night and keeps the last 14. From time to time the owner also takes a full copy of the database and uploads, kept on the owner's own computer for up to 90 days. Backups are used only to recover from a fault. A closed account is not restored from a backup; its details drop out of the backups as they expire.

Server records

Like any website, Strider's server records each request it receives: the IP address, the page or action requested, the time and the browser's description of itself. These records are used only for security and to fix faults, and are kept only as long as that needs, normally no more than 90 days.

Diagnostic reports

The diagnostic report Strider can produce is redacted before it is written: access tokens, sign-in codes and secrets are removed and reported only as present or missing. The report is shown to you before you send it, and nothing is transmitted unless you choose to send it.

Sensitive data

Strider holds no payment card details, no government identifiers, and no health, biometric or special category data.

When an account is closed, a short note that an account was deleted, and when, is kept as proof that it was done. It holds no email address, only a scrambled reference that cannot be turned back into one.

How to delete it

  1. Delete an upload from your library at any time. A file a scheduled post is still waiting on is kept until that post is sent or cancelled.
  2. Disconnect an account inside Strider. Its access token and its post figures are deleted immediately and Strider can no longer publish to it. Posts already scheduled for it are kept, and fail if nothing is connected when they are due.
  3. Revoke access at the platform. Every platform lets you remove an app's access from its own settings. For Google and YouTube this is the Google security settings page. For Facebook, including signing in with Facebook, it is Settings & privacy → Settings → Apps and websites, where you can remove Strider. This works whether or not you still use Strider, and it is separate from Strider's own deletion of the data it holds.
  4. Close your Strider account yourself, under Your account → Delete your account. Everything above is deleted straight away, including your uploads, and drops out of backups as they expire. Cancel a paid plan first; Strider will remind you. If you cannot sign in, email hello@getstrider.co.uk from the address on your account.

6. Your rights

Under UK data protection law you may ask for a copy of the personal data we hold about you, ask for it to be corrected or deleted, object to how it is used, or ask us to restrict its use. Email hello@getstrider.co.uk or use the contact form, and we will respond within one month.

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your data you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

7. Where data is held

Strider's server, with your account, uploads and records, is in London. Some suppliers (email, payments and the public website's host) may process data outside the UK, including in the United States. Where they do, the safeguards UK data protection law requires are used, such as the UK–US data bridge or the UK's standard contract clauses.

10. Forms on our website

This section covers the forms on our public website, which are separate from the Strider application described above.

What we collect. If you use the contact form we collect your email address, your message, and your name if you give it. If you apply to be a Strider ambassador we collect your name, brand name, email address, the social profiles and links you give us, your main platform, audience size (if you give it), the kind of content you make, your preferred route and your reasons for applying.

Why. To reply to messages you send us and to consider and reply to ambassador applications. Nothing else. We do not sell it, share it with advertisers, or add you to anything you did not ask for.

Your permission. For ambassador applications you give it by ticking the box on the form, and you can withdraw it at any time by asking us to remove your details.

How it is handled. Your details are not stored in Strider’s database. The form sends them to us as an email, and that email is the only copy we keep.

How long we keep it. Contact messages: as long as needed to deal with them. Ambassador applications: if you join the programme, for as long as you are an ambassador; if not, until we have told you the outcome, or until you ask us to remove it, whichever comes first.

The early access list. Before public signup opened we kept an early access list (name, email address and mobile number). That list is closed. If you joined it, we keep your details only until we have told you signup is open, or until you ask us to remove them.

Removing your details. Use the contact form with the email address you gave us and ask to be removed. We will delete your details and confirm when it is done.
